1. Research Question

Among Medicare fee-for-service beneficiaries diagnosed with non-metastatic oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (OPSCC), how does TORS (transoral robotic surgery) compare to radiation-based therapy with respect to overall survival and functional morbidity (dysphagia, gastrostomy tube placement, SLP utilization)? Three clinically distinct comparisons are made:

Comparison A TORS alone vs RT alone

TORS alone: patients who received TORS within 12 months of diagnosis — no chemotherapy and no radiation in the same window.

RT alone: patients who received radiation within 12 months of diagnosis — no chemotherapy and no TORS.

Clinical rationale: Compares primary surgical resection to definitive radiation as single-modality treatments for early/favorable OPSCC.

Comparison B TORS + RT vs CRT

TORS + RT: patients who received TORS followed by adjuvant radiation — no chemotherapy in the same window.

CRT: patients who received both chemotherapy and radiation — no TORS in the same window.

Clinical rationale: Compares TORS-based multimodal therapy (surgery + adjuvant RT) to definitive concurrent chemoradiation for locally advanced OPSCC.

Comparison C TORS + CRT vs CRT

TORS + CRT: triple-modality patients who received TORS plus chemotherapy plus radiation within 12 months of diagnosis.

CRT: same as Comparison B control arm — chemoradiation without TORS.

Clinical rationale: Both arms receive chemoradiation. The comparison isolates the contribution of surgery on top of CRT — i.e., for a patient already destined to receive CRT, does adding TORS increase functional burden? If the curves overlap or favor TORS+CRT, surgery is not the driver of bad functional outcomes in CRT-class patients; chemo is. Combined with Comp B (where CRT alone has higher G-tube than TORS+RT), the cross-comparison points to chemo, not surgery, as the dominant driver of late functional morbidity.

3.2 HNC Diagnosis Confirmation

Raw HNC diagnosis claims are extracted from all three claim sources (inpatient, outpatient, carrier) for ICD-10 prefixes C00–C14 and C30–C32. The confirmation algorithm (hnc_confirmed.sql) requires:

A At least one inpatient claim with HNC as principal diagnosis, or
B At least two outpatient/carrier claims with HNC diagnosis, spaced ≥30 days apart

This mirrors the Warren-type algorithm for cancer case ascertainment in claims data. The earliest qualifying claim date becomes first_hnc_date.

3.3 OPSCC Identification

From the confirmed HNC universe, patients are restricted to those with at least one raw diagnosis claim at an OPSCC primary site:

ICD-10 PrefixSite
C01Base of tongue
C09Tonsil
C10Oropharynx (other and unspecified)
C14Lip, oral cavity, and pharynx — ill-defined sites (overlapping)

3.4 Enrollment & Eligibility

1 FFS Medicare enrollment at diagnosis
Must be enrolled in fee-for-service (FFS) Medicare in the month of first_hnc_date.
FFS month defined as: BUYIN = '3' (Part A+B) AND HMOIND IN ('0','4') (no HMO).
Post-diagnosis FFS dropout is handled by censoring in opscc_ffs_dates.sql rather than an upfront consecutive-month requirement.

3.5 Exclusion Criteria

X1 Metastatic disease
Any diagnosis of C78% or C79% in any claim within ±90 days of first_hnc_date. Applied at the opscc_propensity.sql step (has_metastatic_dx = FALSE).
X1b Pre-diagnosis chemo or RT (strict); pre-diagnosis TORS >30 days (loose)
Patients with first_chemo_date or first_rt_date recorded before their first_hnc_date are excluded entirely. Such records almost certainly reflect treatment for a prior unrelated cancer (e.g., chemo for breast cancer years earlier), not OPSCC therapy. For TORS, a window of 30 days before first_hnc_date is allowed because TORS itself is often the diagnostic procedure — pathology from the excision establishes the OPSCC diagnosis, and the billed first_hnc_date often follows the surgery by days to weeks. Patients with TORS more than 30 days before first_hnc_date are excluded as suggestive of an unrelated prior procedure. Applied at opscc_propensity.sql.
X1c Chemo before TORS
Patients with both TORS and chemotherapy where chemo preceded TORS are excluded. This pattern represents either induction-chemo-then-surgery sequences or salvage TORS after CRT failure — both distinct from the intended "TORS + adjuvant CRT" pathway (surgery first, then chemoradiation). Applied at opscc_propensity.sql. Affects the TORS + CRT arm only.
X2 Treatment group = "Other"
Patients who do not fit any of the five protocol-defined groups (e.g., chemotherapy alone, TORS + chemotherapy without radiation) are assigned tx_group = 'Other' and excluded from all PSM analyses. They are retained in opscc_propensity for descriptive purposes.
X3 C77 (nodal disease) — Comparison A only
Patients with any C77% code (nodal metastasis) in inpatient/outpatient/carrier claims within ±90 days of diagnosis are excluded from Comparison A at the PSM stage (iptw_analysis.py filters has_nodal_dx == False before matching). C77 patients are retained in Comparison B and Comparison C, where nodal disease is expected in the multimodal treatment population.

5. Comorbidity Scoring

Elixhauser comorbidity flags are computed via opscc_elixhauser.sql using the Quan et al. ICD-10-CM adaptation (see comorbidity.py for code lists). The lookback window is 12 months before first_hnc_date (exclusive), applied across all three claim sources. Index HNC codes are excluded by the lookback window.

The van Walraven summary score is computed from the 31 Elixhauser flags using the weights from van Walraven et al. (2009). It is used as a continuous covariate in the propensity model and as a binary stratification variable (VW ≤0 = Low, VW >0 = High) in subgroup analyses.

Analysis: Kaplan-Meier + log-rank; Cox PH adjusted for age, sex, race, van Walraven score

7.2 Secondary Outcomes

Dysphagia SECONDARY

Definition: New ICD-10-CM diagnosis of R131x after first_tx_date

ICD-10-CM: R131* (prefix match, LEFT(code,4)='R131')

Sources: Inpatient, outpatient, carrier dx codes

Table: opscc_outcomes (step 9)

Gastrostomy Tube placement SECONDARY

Framework: Multi-interval cumulative incidence of new G-tube placement procedures anchored on first treatment date. Placement procedure codes only — Z93.1 status diagnosis and exchange CPTs are excluded from the primary metric (Z93.1 is unreliable; it re-fires at any encounter while a tube exists, inflating rates in arms with high pre-existing tube prevalence).

Primary flags: Binary placement_by_{T} for T ∈ {14d, 30d, 90d, 180d, 365d, 1095d} indicating at least one placement procedure in [first_tx_date, first_tx_date + T]. Reported both unconditionally and at-risk-conditionally (denominator restricted to patients alive and FFS-enrolled at T).

Sensitivity flags: event_by_{T} using the broader code set (placement + exchange + Z93.1) preserved in the SQL output for sensitivity analysis only — not used in forest plot, primary tables, or cumulative-incidence figure.

Pre-treatment baseline: pre_placement_any covering [dx_date, first_tx_date) — reported as descriptor of baseline severity, not used as exclusion.

Delayed placement (marker for late swallowing failure / dependence): Anchored on treatment completion (tx_completion_date = last RT/chemo date, falling back to surgery date for TORS-alone) rather than treatment start. A "delayed" placement is one occurring ≥90 days after completion (washout that clears the acute mucositis-recovery window). Flags delayed_placement_by_{180,365,730,1095}d give cumulative incidence at 6mo/1yr/2yr/3yr post-completion. Reported two ways: all-comers (every matched patient) and incident (denominator restricted to patients who reached completion+90d with no prior tube, via placement_through_completion90 = FALSE). Same framework applied to SLP (delayed_slp_by_{T}) and dysphagia (delayed_dysphagia_by_{T}).

Primary code set (placement procedures only): CPT 43246, 49440 | HCPCS B4087, B4088 | ICD-10-PCS 0DH60UZ, 0DH63UZ

Sensitivity-only codes: CPT 43760, 43762, 43763, 49450 (exchange/maintenance) | Z93.1 (gastrostomy status diagnosis, 25 slots)

Sources: Inpatient PCS, outpatient PCS, carrier line (HCPCS), outpatient revenue (HCPCS)

Table: opscc_gtube_dependence (step 14, post-PSM). Forest-plot canonical timepoint: placement_by_90d.

SLP Utilization SECONDARY

Framework: Same multi-interval cumulative-incidence framework as G-tube.

Cumulative-incidence flags: Binary event_by_{T} for the same six time boundaries, plus pre-treatment baseline columns.

HCPCS: 92521, 92522, 92523 (eval), 92507, 92526, 97129, 97130 (treatment), 92610, 92611, 92612, 92616, 92617 (swallowing) | Revenue center: 0440–0449

7.4 Pre-existing Outcomes Handling

The G-tube and SLP analyses use a dx-anchored multi-interval framework that surfaces pre-treatment events as explicit columns (pre_event_any, pre_event_count) rather than as exclusion criteria. Pre-treatment events cover the window [dx_date, first_tx_date) and are reported as a baseline-severity descriptor.

For dysphagia (opscc_outcomes.sql), the legacy pre_existing rule is retained: any code appearing in [first_tx_date − 365d, first_tx_date) sets that outcome field to NULL.

8. Statistical Analysis

All analyses loop over COMPARISONS = [('A', 'psm_matched_A', ...), ('B', 'psm_matched_B', ...), ('C', 'psm_matched_C', ...)]. Each comparison filters the matched cohort independently before analysis.

Survival Analysis (survival_analysis.py)

  • Kaplan-Meier: Overall survival curves per comparison (TORS arm vs control arm). Log-rank test for group difference.
  • Cox PH: Adjusted for age and van Walraven score. Stratified by PSM match ID. HR < 1 favors the TORS arm of each comparison.
  • Reported statistics: Median survival, 1-year/3-year/5-year survival probability, HR (95% CI), log-rank p-value.

Functional Outcomes Analysis (outcomes_analysis.py)

  • G-tube placement: Cumulative-incidence proportion of new G-tube placements at each interval (14d, 30d, 90d, 180d, 1-yr, 3-yr) per arm, with 95% Wilson CIs. Reported both unconditionally (treat death/disenrollment as no-event) and at-risk-conditionally. OR + 95% CI computed at the canonical 90d timepoint. Placement procedure codes only — Z93.1 status and exchange CPTs excluded from primary metric.
  • SLP utilization: Cumulative-incidence proportion of any SLP-coded visit at each interval, same intervals and CI framework as G-tube.
  • Dysphagia: ORs from 2×2 contingency tables at each legacy timepoint with the follow-up-eligibility rule (patient counted only if follow-up reached T or event occurred before T).
  • Chi-square test for independence (scipy.stats.chi2_contingency).
  • Pre-treatment events for G-tube/SLP are reported as a baseline descriptor (pre_event_any), not as exclusion criteria.
  • Reference group: control arm of each comparison. OR < 1 favors the TORS-bearing arm.
  • Stratified by: All matched, Age <75, Age ≥75, Low comorbidity (VW ≤0), High comorbidity (VW >0).

11. Key Design Decisions

DecisionRationale
Diagnosis cutoff: June 30, 2023Outpatient revenue file (out_revenuek_all) only available through June 2023; extending beyond this date would leave outpatient-billed RT/chemo codes undetected in H2 2023. Carrier and inpatient files are complete through December 2023.
12-month treatment windowCaptures planned multimodal treatment sequences while excluding late salvage therapy
Warren-type HNC confirmationReduces false positives from incidental or rule-out diagnosis codes in outpatient claims
±90-day metastatic exclusion windowDistinguishes de novo metastatic disease from late recurrence while allowing adequate staging workup time
Pre-existing outcome exclusion (not patient exclusion)Retains sample size; nulling the outcome for pre-existing patients avoids attributing prevalent disease to treatment
Logit PS caliper at 0.2 × SDStandard recommended caliper (Austin 2011); balances match quality vs. sample retention

Triple-therapy arm (Comp C) defined looselyAny TORS + chemo + RT classifies a patient as TORS+CRT. Chemo timing is not constrained to concurrent with RT — this maximizes statistical power for the new arm and reflects the heterogeneous use of chemo in surgical patients in Medicare data.
Comp C compares TORS + CRT to CRT (not to TORS + RT)The original framing was TORS+CRT vs TORS+RT — "does chemo add harm in surgical patients?" That comparison is vulnerable to confounding by indication because chemo is selected for adverse pathology (positive margins, ENE, advanced stage) that Medicare claims can't observe, so the PSM model can't balance disease severity between arms. The current framing — TORS+CRT vs CRT — instead asks "does adding surgery to a CRT course add harm?" Both arms receive chemoradiation, so confounding by chemo indication doesn't bias the comparison; the residual confounding question shifts to surgical eligibility, which is anatomically driven and generally cleaner to interpret. Combined with Comp B (CRT alone vs TORS+RT), the pair of comparisons triangulates whether surgery or chemo drives late functional morbidity.
C77 nodal exclusion for Comp A onlyNodal disease is uncommon in single-modality patients (TORS alone, RT alone) and confounds functional outcomes. In Comp B and Comp C, nodal disease is expected in the multimodal population and is retained.
Multi-interval cumulative-incidence framework for G-tube + SLPThe dataset does not contain DME claims, so true G-tube dependence cannot be measured. Instead, the framework tracks cumulative incidence of any qualifying event at 14d/30d/90d/180d/1-yr/3-yr post-treatment. Persistence of the between-arm gap across multiple intervals is the clinical argument; a single 90d canonical timepoint provides the headline OR.
G-tube primary metric = placement procedure codes onlyThe Z93.1 gastrostomy status diagnosis is unreliable as a primary signal: it re-fires at every routine encounter while a tube exists (chemo infusions, follow-ups, hospitalizations), inflating cumulative incidence in arms with more healthcare contact and high baseline pre-existing tube prevalence (RT alone 11%, CRT 15%). Restricting to placement procedure codes (CPT 43246/49440, HCPCS B4087/B4088, ICD-10-PCS 0DH60UZ/0DH63UZ) ensures the metric reflects new tube placement attributable to treatment, not status re-coding. Exchange CPTs are also excluded because they measure maintenance of existing tubes, not new placement. Z93.1 + exchange flags are preserved in the SQL output as event_by_{T} for sensitivity analysis only.
Pre-treatment events as descriptor, not exclusionFor G-tube and SLP, pre-treatment events (between dx and first treatment) are reported as a baseline-severity descriptor rather than nulling the outcome. This separates baseline-attributable burden from treatment-attributable burden and avoids losing informative patients.
Expanded G-tube code set (added exchange CPTs)Initial placement codes alone overcount peri-treatment tubes that are later pulled. Adding exchange CPTs (43760, 43762, 43763, 49450) captures the small but specific signal of tubes that remained in use long enough to require replacement.
Van Walraven binary split for subgroupsVW ≤0 (Low) vs VW >0 (High) provides a clinically interpretable comorbidity dichotomy; the OPSCC Medicare population is relatively healthy, making tertiles sparsely populated in the highest group
SLP and G-tube SQLs scoped to matched patients onlyClaims extraction is computationally expensive (scans carrier, outpatient, SNF, HHA, inpatient). Restricting to PSM-matched patients (across A, B, and C) avoids scanning claims for patients who will not appear in any analysis.

11.1 Known Limitations

  • No DME claims: The database lacks enteral formula/supply HCPCS codes (B4034–B4162, B9002 returned zero hits). True ongoing G-tube dependence (active feeding) cannot be measured. The cumulative-incidence framework measures new placement procedures, not active dependence. A patient who had a tube placed at day 30 and used it for years registers identically to one who had it placed and removed within weeks.
  • Z93.1 coding-intensity bias (addressed by primary metric): The Z93.1 status diagnosis is re-coded at every healthcare encounter. CRT patients have more encounters (chemo infusions, toxicity admits) and higher baseline pre-existing tube rates (~15%), which would inflate Z93.1-based cumulative incidence even without true differences in new tube placement. To avoid this artifact, the primary G-tube metric restricts to placement procedure codes only. Z93.1 + exchange-based event_by_{T} flags remain in the SQL output as a sensitivity column.
  • Removal codes uninformative: CPT 43870 and ICD-PCS removal codes are billed for only ~1 patient in the matched cohort — the absence-of-removal heuristic cannot be used as a signal of ongoing tube presence.
  • Sample-size attrition at late timepoints: Differential mortality (CRT < TORS in 5-yr OS) reduces denominators at 1-yr and 3-yr cumulative-incidence boundaries. Both unconditional and at-risk-conditional rates are reported so readers can see the effect.
  • Treatment-completion anchor is course-capped: last_rt_date / last_chemo_date are the latest RT/chemo claims within 12 months of diagnosis, matching the treatment-group definition window. This prevents later salvage/recurrence therapy from being mistaken for the index course, but a single 12-month window can still, rarely, contain an early recurrence that would push the completion anchor slightly later than the true definitive-treatment end.
  • Delayed-placement washout is a convention: The ≥90-day washout after completion is chosen to clear the acute mucositis-recovery window (~6–12 weeks); placements between completion and completion+90d are treated as part of the acute/subacute course, not delayed failure. The incident view (no tube through completion+90d) is the cleaner "new late dependence" signal; the all-comers view additionally captures patients who had an acute tube and a separate later placement.
